Web Applications

Home Page

Wheels N' Works Interface

The Home Page gives an overview of workshops, services, and brand details. Users can book appointments instantly without creating an account, and can also access social media links and explore brand information easily.

Non-Customer Management System

Non-customers are assigned a default customer ID and receive appointment details via email, including updates. They can browse services and book a shop, but system IDs (customer, appointment, invoice) are auto-generated and read-only. After an appointment is invoiced, it can only be viewed, not edited or deleted.

Customer Management System

Customers can register, log in, and manage their profile. They can book appointments and edit or delete only current bookings—not past ones. Once an appointment is invoiced, it becomes view-only, and the invoice appears in their profile.

Shop Owners-Admin Management System

Approved shop owners receive login details and can manage their shop info, services, appointments, and invoices. They can book appointments for customers, add service charges, and send or download invoices as PDF. After invoicing, appointments become view-only, and only invoice cost/payment updates are allowed.

Super Admin Dashboard

Super Admin can manage all shops—add, edit, or delete shop owners—and view system insights such as total appointments, top services, top-earning shops, invoice counts, payment status, and non-customer bookings.

Mail Services

The mails you receive while booking and signing up as admin and invoices of service.

WALDEN: Low-Cost AI Educational Companion for Government Schools

📅 2024 - Present
🏛️ Independent Research - Hyderabad, India
👥 Principal Investigator
👨‍🎓 200-Student Study

Research Gap Identified:

Most existing research focuses on high-cost or laboratory-based robotic systems, which are not always practical for everyday classrooms. There is limited exploration of low-cost, emotionally engaging, and student-friendly robots designed specifically to support learning and motivation. This gap highlights the need for innovative approaches, such as our WALDEN-inspired educational companion, which aims to combine affordability, affective interaction, and educational effectiveness.

Research Questions

RQ1: Is it possible to make a low-budget AI tutor for kids instead of giving unrestricted devices?

→ Addresses a real educational technology gap

RQ2: Does embodying AI in a beloved character attract kids and make learning fun?

→ Tests embodied cognition theory in educational contexts

RQ3: Is restricted AI good or boring for educational purposes?

→ Challenges the "more features = better" assumption

RQ4: How beneficial is this system? Are kids actually gaining knowledge?

→ Measures real educational outcomes

Current Status

IRB approval is currently in progress for a 200-student quasi-experimental study across government schools in Hyderabad. Preliminary pilot testing with 30 students showed significant engagement improvements and knowledge retention in basic AI concepts, mathematics, and environmental science.

Methodology

Mixed-methods approach combining quantitative pre/post-test assessments with qualitative student interviews and teacher observations. Control groups will use traditional learning methods while intervention groups interact with WALDEN for structured lessons.

Expected Impact

This research aims to demonstrate that thoughtful design and cost-efficient engineering can yield learning outcomes comparable to expensive commercial educational systems, making quality AI education accessible to underserved populations at less than 1/50th of the cost.

EcoSort Edu: AI-Powered Environmental Learning System

📅 2024 - Present
🏛️ STEM Education Research
👥 Lead Researcher

Project Overview:

A low-cost educational robotics project that classifies waste using AI image recognition, helping students (ages 10-16) learn environmental science, data science, and engineering through hands-on practice with a four-phase learning workflow: Data Collection → Training → Testing → Application.

Primary Research Questions

RQ1: Learning Effectiveness

How does hands-on interaction with EcoSort Edu affect students' conceptual understanding of AI/machine learning and environmental science compared to traditional instruction methods?

RQ2: Behavioral Impact

To what extent does participation in the EcoSort Edu program influence students' waste-sorting behaviors and environmental attitudes in and beyond the classroom?

Secondary Research Questions

RQ3: Pedagogical Design

What specific design features of the four-phase learning workflow contribute most significantly to learning outcomes?

RQ4: Engagement & Motivation

How do gamification elements and collaborative data collection affect student engagement, persistence, and self-efficacy across different demographic groups?

RQ5: Implementation Factors

What are the key facilitators and barriers to implementing EcoSort Edu in government schools from teachers' perspectives, and what support structures are needed?

RQ6: AI Literacy Development

Can students develop sophisticated understanding of AI concepts (training data, accuracy, bias, limitations) through non-programming-based interaction with the system?

Educational Value

  • Students learn AI/ML concepts through practical application
  • Develops environmental awareness and waste management skills
  • Promotes data literacy without requiring programming knowledge
  • Encourages collaborative learning and scientific thinking

Literature Review: Accessible Educational Robotics

📅 2024
🏛️ Systematic Review
👥 Lead Researcher

Research Focus:

This study identifies critical gaps in current research and emphasizes the need for developing comprehensive tablet-based frameworks that can guide teaching in effectively implementing educational robotics programming through user-friendly and accessible interfaces.

Key Findings from Literature

1. Pedagogical Effectiveness:

STEM education's primary strength lies in its pedagogical methodology, which effectively develops students' creative problem-solving abilities, teamwork competencies, and analytical thinking. Contemporary research findings indicate that classroom integration of robotic technologies has a positive influence on various aspects of student engagement fosters the development of multiple cognitive competencies.

2. Evidence for Educational Robotics:

Evidence-based research confirms that robots capable of diverse expressive behaviors, including musical and kinesthetic performances, create novel and cognitively stimulating enriched environments. Research validates that humanoid robots (NAO) can effectively engage students' natural inquisitiveness and spontaneous learning behaviors.

3. Research Gap - Cost Barriers:

The meta-analytical findings revealed that educational robotics interventions produced measurably positive learning outcomes. However, moderate improvements were observed in academic performance and student attitudes toward learning. The enhancement of problem-solving skills showed limited statistical significance. The research establishes the feasibility of deployment of humanoid robots in academic environments, but findings confirm that NAO robots are extremely expensive.

Critical Gap Identified

However, most existing research still focuses on high-cost or laboratory-based robotic systems, which are not always practical for everyday classrooms.

There is limited exploration of low-cost, emotionally engaging, and student-friendly robots designed specifically to support learning and motivation. This gap highlights the need for innovative approaches, such as our WALDEN-inspired educational companion, which aims to combine:

  • Affordability - Accessible to government schools with limited budgets
  • Affective Interaction - Emotionally engaging character design
  • Educational Effectiveness - Proven learning outcomes

Research Implications

This literature review establishes the theoretical foundation and practical justification for developing low-cost, affective educational robots like WALDEN and EcoSort Edu. By addressing the cost barrier while maintaining pedagogical effectiveness, these systems can democratize access to advanced STEM education in resource-constrained environments.
