Research Problem

Most educational robotics research focuses on expensive systems ($5,000–$30,000) or gives students unrestricted devices. There's limited exploration of affordable, education-restricted AI systems that work alongside teachers.

Innovation

WALDEN provides voice-based AI tutoring at under $5 per student/year (including hardware amortization and AI costs) — over 99% cheaper than commercial alternatives. Physically inspired by the Disney character WALL-E through 3D-printed design, WALDEN uses familiar character aesthetics to reduce anxiety and create a friendly "buddy" dynamic — particularly effective for young children.

❓ Research Questions

RQ1: Immediate Learning Gains (Primary) What is the magnitude of immediate learning gains (effect size, Cohen's d) produced by WALDEN robot interactions across four consecutive daily sessions, measured through paired pre/post knowledge tests in elementary students (Grades 1-8, ages 6-13) in government schools?

RQ2: Knowledge Retention Do students demonstrate retention of learned content one week post-intervention, and what is the decay rate (percentage retention) compared to immediate post-test scores, analyzed by age cohort (Ages 6-8, 9-13)?

RQ3: Age-Cohort Differences Are there statistically significant differences in learning gains and retention rates across two age cohorts (Ages 6-8, 9-13), as measured through Mann-Whitney U tests and ANOVA?

RQ4: Engagement & Preference What percentage of students report preferring WALDEN interactions over their prior tablet-based learning experiences, based on self-report survey responses, and how does stated preference correlate with measured learning gains?

RQ5: Cost-Effectiveness What is the cost per measurable learning gain (dollars per effect size unit) for WALDEN compared to commercial educational robots (NAO, Pepper), and what is the scalability potential for government school adoption?

RQ6: Teacher Perspectives What are teachers' assessments of WALDEN's classroom feasibility, student engagement impact, and pedagogical value based on structured observation forms across 32 sessions?

RQ7: Session-by-Session Learning Trajectory Do students demonstrate cumulative learning gains across four consecutive sessions, or do learning effects plateau after initial sessions? What is the optimal intervention duration?

Expanded Study Design

  • Sample: 280 students (ages 6–13, Grades 1–8) across 3 government schools
  • Duration: 4-day intervention + 1-week retention testing
  • Measurements: Pre/post/retention MCQ tests (10 questions per cohort)
  • Design: Within-subjects quasi-experimental, two age cohorts (6-8 years, 9-13 years)
  • Additional Data: Engagement surveys, teacher feedback, system logs

Session Structure (per day — 45 minutes)

  • Pre-test (10 min): Curriculum-aligned MCQ assessment
  • WALDEN interaction (15 min): Student-led educational queries
  • Teacher-guided discussion (10 min): Concept reinforcement
  • Post-test (10 min): Immediate learning gain measurement
  • Retention test (1 week later): Knowledge retention assessment

Measures

Quantitative:

  • Learning assessments: Pre/post/retention tests (10 questions per cohort)
  • Student surveys: 8-item engagement scale (ages 6-8), 17-item engagement scale (ages 9-13)
  • Technical metrics: Speech recognition accuracy, interaction success rates, cost per interaction

Qualitative:

  • Teacher observation forms (structured, after each session)
  • Teacher evaluation survey (comprehensive, after Day 4)

Cost Breakdown

  • Hardware: ~$50 per robot
  • AI Costs: under $2.50 per student/year (based on pilot usage patterns)
  • Infrastructure: Uses existing school WiFi (no additional costs)
  • Total: under $5 per student/year vs. $5,000+ for commercial robots

🔍 Critical Gap Identified

Problem: Most existing research focuses on high-cost, laboratory-based social robots (e.g., NAO robots costing $5,000+). These systems are impractical for everyday classrooms and often attempt to replace the teacher, which can negatively influence students’ emotional engagement, reduce social interaction, and disrupt their sense of safety—particularly for students who are introverted or socially anxious.

Gap: There is limited research on low-cost, emotionally engaging, student-friendly educational robots that support teachers, maintain a safe classroom environment, and promote positive affective connections without replacing human interaction.

Solution direction: Develop an educational robot that integrates affordability, affective interaction, and pedagogical effectiveness — a cute, classroom-friendly assistant designed to enhance teacher–student engagement while preserving emotional safety and comfort.

📊 Key Findings

  • Robotics improves engagement and cognitive skills
  • Evidence supports measurable learning outcomes
  • High costs limit adoption in real classrooms
  • Strong need for affordable alternatives
